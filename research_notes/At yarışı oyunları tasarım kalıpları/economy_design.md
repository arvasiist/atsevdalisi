## 1. Main income sources (faucets) and main costs (sinks)

### Takeaway
Across the genre, income comes from race purses (PvP or vs-AI), horse sales/claims, breeding/stud fees, betting (single-player sims), and daily/achievement/event rewards. Costs are race entry fees, horse purchases, jockeys/staff, training, facilities, and breeding. Premium currency is used for rare genetics and convenience. Only the vs-AI games (Rival Stars, Umamusume) have a true **house faucet** from racing. The player-funded PvP games (ZED RUN, Photo Finish LIVE) move money between players and take a cut. Real-world racing is heavily negative-EV for owners and is subsidised from outside: owners' entry fees make up only ~14% of UK prize money.

### Cited Findings
**Real-world benchmark (useful for the "realism vs fun" trade-off)**
- UK 2025: record prize money of **£194.7m** (+3.5% YoY). Owners paid **£26.8m in entry fees = 14% of all prize money**. The other ~86% comes from outside owners' pockets. — [Racing Post, 2025](https://www.racingpost.com/news/britain/record-prize-money-in-britain-in-2025-as-3.5-per-cent-rise-produces-total-of-194.7-million-aemHQ0P9zNkP/)
- BHA research (undated in snippet): because prize money is unequally distributed, an owner of a *modestly talented* Flat horse can expect to lose **92p of every £1 invested**. — [Racing Post](https://racingpost.com/news/how-much-do-owners-make-or-lose-as-the-case-may-be/423074)
- UK owners recover "somewhere under **25%**" of running costs through prize money. France recovers **40–50%**. Another source says UK recovery is "creeping up towards **30%**" (undated; attribution approximate). — [Racing Post, "How to save racehorse ownership"](https://racingpost.com/news/rp-sunday/how-to-save-racehorse-ownership-part-one/286185)
- Racehorse Owners Association survey (2015 or 2016; the sources disagree on the year): average annual cost of a Flat horse **£22,595**, a jumps horse **£16,325**. The 2010 survey gave **£20,264** for Flat. (attribution approximate) — [Thoroughbred Owner & Breeder](https://theownerbreeder.com/?p=12130); [ROA](https://www.roa.co.uk/asset/E58D6272%2D761D%2D4779%2D83AC65DEA9B6DDA4/)
- An academic study of horse breeding economics found the **selling price of the trained horse** is the decisive factor for profitability, with cost changes mattering far less. It likened breeding to "a gamble or playing the lottery". — [AgEcon Search (Fuchs et al.)](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/345811)

**General F2P faucet/sink taxonomy**
- Common faucets: level completion, daily login bonuses, quests, achievements, rewarded ads. Common sinks: upgrade costs, marketplace fees, cosmetic purchases, consumables. — [Metavert](https://metavert.io/game-economy-design); [MWM glossary](https://mwm.ai/glossary/game-economy)
- Every economy is built from **currencies, faucets, sinks and converters**. — [Game Growth Advisor, Jul 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-07-14-game-economy-design-virtual-currency-balancing/)

### Inferences
- Real racing is economically viable for owners only because ~86% of prize money is external (in the UK: betting levy, media rights, racecourses). A game whose prize pools are funded **only** by players' entry fees reproduces the "lose 92p per £1" owner experience unless it injects house-funded "added money" (quests, season rewards, vs-AI purses). This matches what the successful vs-AI games do (Rival Stars, Umamusume).
- The PvP real-money games (ZED RUN, Photo Finish) make the platform's money mostly from **breeding and primary horse sales**, not racing. For a soft-currency game this suggests breeding/stud fees and primary-market horse sales (e.g., a breed shop) are natural **hard sinks**, which lets racing itself be less taxed.
- Starters Orders shows that a single-player sim can lean on **betting** as a faucet. In a multiplayer soft-currency game, betting on other players' races would create a parallel economy that needs its own rake and limits.

## 2. Entry fee vs prize structures: places paid, rake, EV, AI fillers

### Takeaway
Player-funded PvP horse games pay the **top 3 (ZED RUN: 60/25/15)** or the **top 5 (Photo Finish: 60% to 1st, rest across 2nd–5th)** and scale the purse with actual entrants. The house cut ranges from **0% (ZED RUN, 2021)** to **about 18–20% (Photo Finish, to track owners)**. In all-human pools, average EV is necessarily negative (zero-sum minus rake), so the games rely on outside value (NFT appreciation, breeding, tournaments, new-player freerolls) to keep the average player engaged. No source described how any game handles an AI filler's share of a purse.

### Cited Findings
- **ZED RUN (2021, older):** racing was described as **rake-less**, with horses placing **3rd or better splitting the whole pot**. — [Naavik, Dec 2021](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/). A podcast guide says the split is **60/25/15** and that ZED "used to take a small rake but now it's split evenly down the line". — [Sports Gambling Podcast](https://www.sportsgamblingpodcast.com/?p=106756)
- ZED RUN entry fees were typically **$2–$15** (2021). — [User Mag](https://usermag.co/p/digital-horses-are-the-talk-of-the). Beta buy-ins ranged **$0.50–$100**, with larger buy-ins giving larger pools. — [ZED RUN Medium, beta racing](https://medium.com/@zed_run/zed-beta-racing-726bb73f05e7)
- **Conflict:** one 2021 article says ~**95%** of a ~$200 pot went to winners on $5–$25 entries, which implies a ~5% cut and contradicts "rake-less" (attribution approximate). — [Stockhead](https://stockhead.com.au/?p=136823) vs [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/)
- ZED RUN later added a **tournament system**. — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/ca/zed-run-introduces-new-tournament-system). **Zed Champions** races horses **automatically, matching them with horses of similar skill**, so players no longer enter races manually. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/316789/ethereum-nft-game-zed-run-reborn-base)
- **Photo Finish LIVE:** the purse and payout depend on the number of entrants. The listed maximum purse shrinks if fewer horses enter. The standard split is **60% to 1st, the rest shared by 2nd–5th**, and some races split more evenly depending on competition level. — [Third Time Games](https://thirdtimegames.com/real-money-racing/); [Third Time Games: Competing on a Budget](https://thirdtimegames.com/?p=7043) (attribution approximate)
- Photo Finish: **20% of all race entry fees** (in $DERBY) go to **track owners**, with no Third Time platform fee on that share. This applied during a ~12-month "growth phase" ending **May 2024**. — [Third Time Games: Photo Finish LIVE](https://thirdtimegames.com/photo-finish-live/). A third-party post says the other **80% goes to winners**. — [Horse Racing Capital (Medium)](https://medium.com/@horse-racing-capital/photo-finish-live-tokenomics-8ec6cb233455). **Conflict:** a token-holder post says the rake comes **out of the winning purse** ("only the winners are being taxed") and is "subject to change". — [YB the Commish (Medium)](https://medium.com/@ybthecommish/photo-finish-live-crown-1eae152a271d). Tracks are licensed per season, and $CROWN stakers share the track's fees. — [photofinish.live/tracks](https://photofinish.live/tracks)
- A Photo Finish community proposal (Season 24, **Feb 2025**) names the key problem of horse racing games as **one "monster" horse winning every race and taking everyone's money**. — [Bukidnon Farms proposal (Notion)](https://bukidnon-farms-pfl.notion.site/Crown-and-Purse-Distribution-Proposal-Economic-Growth-now-18ff0c93488a806385adf433925e68d6)
- A Photo Finish user review warns there is "a skill curve… you may lose some money in the process". — [Skribr / Wisenode guide](https://skribr.io/app/article/the-ultimate-photo-finish-live-guide-part-1/) (attribution approximate)
- **Rival Stars** (vs-AI): the prize changes "based on the horses in the race", so the purse is a house-funded faucet scaled by field strength. — [Rival Stars wiki: Races](https://rival-star-horse-racing-player-guide.fandom.com/wiki/Races)
- **Real-world prize splits (for reference):** UK Flat prize funds go ~**80% to owners, 7.85% to trainers, 5.77% to jockeys, 4.75% to stable staff, 1.63% to industry causes**. Jumps owners get 78.25% and jockeys 7.52% (BHA owners' toolkit, per search summary; attribution approximate). — [Racing Club guide](https://racingclub.com/guides/syndicates-and-ownership/how-racehorse-prize-money-works-and-how-syndicates-split-it/). Melbourne Cup: **85/10/5** owner/trainer/jockey. — [Wikipedia: Melbourne Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cup). UK Flat jockey riding fee **£173.54 per ride**. — [Hambleton Racing](https://www.hambletonracing.co.uk/how-much-do-jockeys-get-paid/) (attribution approximate). Kentucky Derby: **$25,000 to enter + $25,000 to start**. — [TwinSpires](https://www.twinspires.com/edge/racing/kentucky-derby/026-kentucky-derby-purse-how-much-prize-money-will-the-winning-owner-and-trainer-earn-may-2/). Minnesota rules: entry money is **not refunded** on death or failure to start. — [Minnesota Revisor](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/pdf/7883/2025-04-16%2010:00:32+00:00)

### Inferences
- **Effective Photo Finish rake:** $9.7M to track owners ÷ $54.7M entry fees ≈ **17.7%**, which is consistent with the stated ~20% (computed from the [DappRadar](https://dappradar.com/blog/photo-finish-live-horse-racing-game-solana) figures; the timing windows of the two numbers may differ).
- **Share of field paid:** ZED RUN paid 3 of 12 (25% of the field), Photo Finish pays 5 of its field. Concentrating ~60% on the winner, plus the "monster horse" problem, means the median player loses money every race. Wider payouts (or a partial refund for near-misses) reduce variance for average players but cannot change the zero-sum-minus-rake total.
- **EV rule of thumb:** in an all-human pool with rake r, the average entrant's EV is −r × fee. To make racing ≥ 0 EV for the *average* player, the house must add money equal to at least the rake (and any bot residual) through added purses, quests or season prizes. Otherwise the population drifts toward the "owners recover <25%" real-world outcome.
- **AI fillers:** none of the sourced games publishes a rule for an AI filler's share. The vs-AI games (Rival Stars) treat the whole purse as a faucet, while the PvP games have no bots. A design where the bot share **burns** (removed from the economy) acts as an extra hidden sink. It prevents a solo player from recapturing the pool but makes thin lobbies strongly negative-EV. This should be made visible in the UI or offset by an added-money faucet. (This is my inference; at least one live design does this, per this project's CLAUDE.md.)
- Auto-matching by skill (Zed Champions) directly targets the "monster horse" problem. Class/level brackets have the same purpose.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) official rake or split table for Photo Finish or Zed Champions was retrievable.
- No source found on how any horse game handles AI filler prize shares (pay to house, redistribute, or burn).
- No published average-player ROI for any game.

## 3. New player experience: starting money, starter horse, first purchase, anti-bankruptcy

### Takeaway
Well-regarded designs give new players a **free but restricted starter horse that can race in free races** (Photo Finish trainer horse, T-Series), new-player **freerolls** (Rookie Rally), and an early-game generosity window. Single-player sims without a safety net produce early-bankruptcy complaints (Starters Orders: loans "only speed up the loss"). Management sims commonly use a **grace-period bankruptcy rule** rather than an instant wipe.

### Cited Findings
- **Photo Finish trainer horse:** every new stable gets a free **Trainer Horse** that may only enter free **T-Series races**. It **cannot breed, be sold or retire**. T-Series races award **points toward an end-of-season leaderboard** instead of $DERBY. — [Third Time Games: Trainer Horses](https://thirdtimegames.com/introducing-photo-finish-live-trainer-horses/); free races sit in a "Free Races" tab. — [Third Time Games](https://thirdtimegames.com/?p=4587) (attribution approximate)
- **Rookie Rally:** every new stable that buys a horse gets a whitelist spot in a **freeroll** race at the end of its first season (Mar 2025). — [Photo Finish on X](https://x.com/photofinishgame/status/1898173525472821462). One Third Time page describes the prize as **$150 in Derby (12,000 DERBY) and 250 Crown in each of four races** for new stables in their first season of paid racing (attribution approximate). — [Third Time Games](https://thirdtimegames.com/?p=4587)
- A 2024 sign-up offer reportedly gave a **real-money horse plus $50 of DERBY** (may no longer apply). — [PaceAdvantage forum](http://www.paceadvantage.com/forum/printthread.php?t=182640)
- **Starters Orders 7:** a new player reports **running out of money early**, and says **taking out loans only speeds up the loss**. Advice from replies: play as an owner rather than a trainer, and use the easier training setting while learning. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/978960/discussions/0/3417684796202138105)
- **Rival Stars:** the paid **Starter Horse Bundle** (a "Future Champion" horse + 500 Gold) is listed at **$4.99 or $8.99**, depending on storefront. — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rival-stars-horse-racing/id1215477266); [App Store NZ](https://apps.apple.com/nz/app/rival-stars-horse-racing/id1215477266). A Steam player says **A/A+ market horses are enough to reach Prestige 20** if you refresh the market until a good horse appears. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1166860/discussions/0/4138312860563917268)
- **Bankruptcy grace periods (management-game comparables):** Battrick (a Hattrick-style browser manager): once the balance falls below a threshold, the account gets a notice and **14 days to recover**. After that the bank takes control and the account is locked, and a new account needs a two-week wait. A bankrupt franchise team becomes a **bot**, and membership does not protect against bankruptcy. — [Battrick rules](https://www.battrick.org/nl/rules.asp?item=10). Hattrick (2003 forum guide, older and regional): the debt limit was **AU$1,000,000**, with **five logins** to recover before the team was declared bankrupt. — [FOFC forum archive](https://forums.operationsports.com/fofc/archive/index.php/t-14549.html)
- **Generous-first-session principle:** modern F2P games are generous with currency and content from the first session and push monetization only after players have tried the game. A first purchase works better when its value builds over time; front-loading can make paying players burn through currency and quit. — [MobileFreeToPlay: Making the First Purchase](https://mobilefreetoplay.com/free-to-play-monetization-making-the-first-purchase/)
- **Starter packs** are one-time, discounted deals for new players that improve onboarding and early engagement (vendor guidance). — [Unity docs: Starter Packs](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/services/solutions/starter-packs). Giving the first monetized item free is a "soft" introduction to monetization (Roblox example). — [GameRefinery onboarding part 3](https://gamerefinery.com/soft-landing-to-conversion-introducing-onboarding-best-practices-part-3)
- **Pacing guideline:** the early game should be source-heavy, the mid-game near equilibrium, and the late game slightly sink-heavy (attribution approximate). — [0xWolf Substack](https://0xwolf.substack.com/p/how-to-create-a-sustainable-game)

### Inferences
- The Photo Finish trainer horse is a strong template for a **bankruptcy floor**. A free, unsellable, non-breedable horse that can always run free races (rewarding points or small capped soft currency) ensures a player can never be locked out of the core loop. Making it unsellable prevents multi-account farming of the free horse.
- New-player freerolls (no entry fee, house-funded purse) are the PvP-safe way to give a positive-EV first experience without distorting the main pools.
- Debt plus loans hurt beginners (Starters Orders). A soft-currency game should avoid loans, or cap them tightly, and should not let balances go negative.
- Restricting the free starter horse to the weakest breed or tier (as this project does) matches Photo Finish's approach, **provided** there is a fair bracket for it to win in (a free/rookie class).

### Gaps
- No verified "time to first meaningful purchase" metric for any horse game.
- No starting-cash numbers for Starters Orders, Rival Stars, Howrse or Horse Racing Manager.
- No conversion-rate data for starter packs. Sources are practitioner and vendor guidance only.

## 4. Upkeep and maintenance costs as sinks; chores and frustration

### Takeaway
Upkeep works as a sink but turns into resentment when it feels like a **tax or a mandatory daily chore** (Howrse pays only if you log in and care for a horse; stamina timers in Umamusume). The literature favours **voluntary, aspirational sinks** and **percentage-based sinks** over flat recurring "repair taxes". Real-world upkeep (~£16–23k per horse per year in the UK) is the reason real ownership loses money, and it is not a fun template to copy literally.

### Cited Findings
- **Howrse:** daily Equus pay requires logging in and caring for at least one horse. — [Howrse Helper Guide](http://thehowrsehelperguide.weebly.com/equus--passes.html)
- **Umamusume:** stamina gates are TP 30 per Career (regen 1 per 10 min) and RP (regen 1 per 120 min). Carat refills are the "last resort". — [Deltia's Gaming: TP](https://deltiasgaming.com/?p=280586). Team Trials are recommended daily because victory rewards drop after matches. — [Goonhammer](https://www.goonhammer.com/understanding-team-trials-in-umamusume-pretty-derby)
- **Rival Stars grind:** one objective required **79 races**. The game "gets fairly grindy the further up you get", though auto-running makes non-competitive races low-effort. — [Aywren blog, Sep 2024](https://aywren.com/2024/09/17/rival-stars-horse-racing-desktop-many-races-to-run). Common Sense Media: the game is "so much about everything but the racing that it ends up being rather dull". — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/rival-stars-horse-racing). Pure Nintendo (7/10) finds the gameplay repetitive over time. — [Pure Nintendo](https://purenintendo.com/review-rival-stars-horse-racing-nintendo-switch/)
- **Hard vs soft sinks:** hard sinks permanently destroy currency (consumables, repair costs, crafting materials, fast-travel fees, respec). Soft sinks change its form (cosmetics, upgrades with salvage value). Aspirational sinks are the most effective inflation control because players voluntarily hoard toward them. **Repair costs that feel like a tax create resentment.** (attribution approximate) — [AlterLab GameForge economy-designer skill](https://openskillindex.com/skills/alterlab-ieu-alterlab-gameforge-game-economy-designer)
- **Fixed sinks lose bite as wealth grows** (a 100-gold potion is meaningless to a millionaire). **Percentage-based sinks** (transaction taxes, value-indexed repair) stay effective across the lifecycle. Player-to-player sales only transfer value; NPC purchases and fees destroy it. (attribution approximate) — [PulseGeek: inflation causes & fixes](https://pulsegeek.com/articles/inflation-in-video-game-economies-causes-and-fixes/)
- **Balance warning:** too many faucets cause inflation that destroys the value of effort, and too many sinks cause frustration that pushes players away. — [Metavert](https://metavert.io/game-economy-design); [Unity economy guide](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide)
- **Real-world:** UK annual cost per horse is ~£22,595 Flat and £16,325 jumps (2015/16 ROA survey; attribution approximate). — [Thoroughbred Owner & Breeder](https://theownerbreeder.com/?p=12130)

### Inferences
- Per-horse **time-based upkeep** (a daily fee whether or not you play) punishes absence and drives churn among casual players. **Activity-based costs** (pay when you train, race or breed) are sinks players choose, which fits the "voluntary sink" advice.
- If upkeep exists, scaling it with **roster size or horse value** (a percentage-style sink) is better than a flat fee: it bites hoarding whales without crushing beginners with one horse.
- Daily-care requirements should give a **bonus for doing** rather than a **penalty for not doing**, which avoids the Howrse "chore" dynamic.

### Gaps
- No sourced numbers for per-horse upkeep in Rival Stars, Howrse or Horse Racing Manager.

## 5. Premium currency policies; pay-to-win criticism and how games avoid it

### Takeaway
The most-criticised horse games put **power** behind premium currency or subscriptions: Rival Stars' Star Club-only stud traits and gold-only rare genes, and Howrse's Passes. Umamusume is a power-creep gacha (pity ≈ 200 pulls = 30,000 Carats) that players still find tolerable because PvP is asynchronous and limited. One-time-purchase editions (Rival Stars on Steam) remove the double currency and are better received. The literature notes that **cosmetics-only** F2P models often struggled, so the realistic "fair" path is **cosmetics + convenience + passes**, with no stat or genetics power for premium currency.

### Cited Findings
**Rival Stars**
- **Star Riding Club** is a monthly auto-renewing subscription with daily Gold, extra horse room, early access to features and barrel racing. Update 1.77 offered a second free trial. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rival-stars-horse-racing/id1215477266)
- Subscribers get a **caretaker automatically hired at a foal's birth**, giving **guaranteed stat point bonuses** and an optional trait change. **Star Club traits are only found on Star Club studs.** — [Rival Stars wiki: Breeding Guide](https://rivalstars-horseracing.fandom.com/wiki/Breeding_Guide)
- App Store reviewers: "almost all the benefits and cool facilities/gear need a membership". One cites **~$30/month** (unverified user claim). Gold perks are "mostly out of reach", rare breeding genes require gold, and gold rewards from play are small. — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rival-stars-horse-racing/id1215477266?see-all=reviews). Gold is called the "pay to play" currency: without it, horses other than Thoroughbreds and Selle Français are hard to get. — [Equine on the Mind](https://equineonthemind.com/quite-possibly-the-best-horse-game-a-rival-stars-horse-racing-review-ios-version/)
- **Steam edition:** a one-time purchase with **no double currency and no subscription**. Premium items are available and cheap. Reviewers disagree on whether the ~$40 price is fair ("a $20 game at most" vs. worth it compared with mobile subscriptions). — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1166860/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated); [Steam "$40 price" thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1166860/discussions/0/2576571096285453224/)

**Umamusume**
- **150 Carats per pull, 1,500 per 10-pull.** Pity takes ~**200 pulls = 30,000 Carats** on a single banner. Leftover points convert to **Clovers**, which cannot buy high-value units. F2P players find 30,000 Carats in one banner (~1 week) tough. — [Deltia's Gaming: Pity](https://deltiasgaming.com/?p=279375); [FDayTalk](https://www.fdaytalk.com/umamusume-pretty-derby-pity-system/)
- A **$6.99** monthly pack gives **500 paid Carats upfront + 50 free Carats daily for 30 days** (price unverified). — [GuruGamer cash shop guide](https://gurugamer.com/mobile-games/cash-shop-guide-for-umamusume-pretty-derby-25100/amp). An informal Reddit estimate puts a 10-pull at about **$15** and worst-case maxing of one card at about **$1,500** (dated, informal; via search summary).

**Howrse**
- After **day 50**, a player can buy **one Pass with 20% of their Equus reserve every 30 days**, a rate-limited soft-to-hard conversion valve. — [Howrse Helper Guide](http://thehowrsehelperguide.weebly.com/equus--passes.html); [Book of Howrse: Passes](http://thebookofhowrse.weebly.com/on-passes.html)

- A German consumer advice centre (2009) cited Howrse as an example in a warning against F2P. A 2011 German TV report alleged a child called a premium number 267 times to buy Passes (single allegation). — [Wikipedia: Howrse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howrse)

**ZED RUN / Photo Finish (real-money)**
- The house's money came from breeding, Genesis sales and **skins** (cosmetics), and racing was rake-less (2021). — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/). **General literature**
- Dual currency exists so free players always have something to earn and payers have something worth buying. Most transactions are priced in soft currency, and hard currency buys more soft currency than faucets give. The **hard-to-soft exchange rate** is a central design question: "get this wrong in either direction and one currency cannibalizes the other". — [MWM glossary](https://mwm.ai/glossary/game-economy); [Game Growth Advisor, 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-07-14-game-economy-design-virtual-currency-balancing/)
- One analysis says most F2P **cosmetics-only** games have either shut down or reworked their monetization. — [Eric Guan, "Dressing Up for the Grocery Store"](https://ericguan.substack.com/p/dressing-up-for-the-grocery-store)
- Battle passes: some players feel negatively about them, and the market is saturated, so players pick which passes to commit to. — [Blizzard Watch, May 2023 (older)](https://blizzardwatch.com/2023/05/17/feel-battle-passes-games/). Academic work on battle-pass and loot-box perception: [Ahonen 2022 thesis, Tampere (older)](https://trepo.tuni.fi/handle/10024/139478)
- Vili Lehdonvirta's GDC talk "Economic Balancing and Improved Monetization Through Clever Sink Design" covers **6 currency sinks and 11 item sinks** and a spreadsheet simulation method (date not shown). — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020085/Economic-Balancing-and-Improved-Monetization)

### Inferences
- **Clearest pay-to-win red flags in this genre:** premium-only breeding genetics or traits (Rival Stars Star Club studs), subscription-guaranteed stat bonuses, and premium-only breeds. These are exactly what reviewers complain about. A gem policy that bans gems from stats, breeds, feed, energy, jockeys and horses avoids all three.
- A **rate-limited soft-to-hard valve** (Howrse: 20% of reserve, once per 30 days) lets dedicated free players reach some premium items without killing gem sales. The **reverse** (gems → chips) is what turns a premium currency into pay-to-win when chips fund prize pools.
- **Umamusume monthly-pass value math:** $6.99 → 500 + 50×30 = **2,000 Carats ≈ 13.3 pulls ≈ $0.52 per pull**, versus about $1.50 per pull at the informal $15-per-10 rate. Monthly passes are the "best value" anchor. Pity (30,000 Carats) ≈ 15 months of the pass alone. In a cosmetics-only gem economy, a small monthly pass (daily gems plus a cosmetic) is the analogous low-friction product.
- Cosmetics-only revenue is risky (per Guan). Convenience items that **do not affect race outcomes** (extra stable slots, cosmetic silks, replay/photo tools, queue convenience) are the usual middle ground, with each item checked against "does this change who wins?".

## 6. Inflation control and long-term economy health

### Takeaway
The standard toolkit is pairing every faucet with a sink, percentage-based fees on trade, aspirational late-game sinks, segment-level monitoring (whales vs new players), and published or internal economic reports (EVE Online's Monthly Economic Report). Horse games add genre-specific risks: **horse oversupply from breeding** and **one dominant horse sweeping purses**. Real-money horse games show what happens when the currency loses value (Photo Finish's $DERBY figures suggest a large devaluation) and when faucets lack sinks (the Axie collapse narrative).

### Cited Findings
- **Faucets > sinks for long → inflation; sinks > faucets → deflation.** — [Medium: Designing Game Economies](https://medium.com/@msahinn21/designing-game-economies-inflation-resource-management-and-balance-fa1e6c894670)
- **Pair every faucet with a matching sink before shipping.** — [Unity game economy guide](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide). Sinks must be sized relative to source generation rates. — [Machinations: F2P economy design](https://machinations.io/articles/game-economy-design-free-to-play-games); [Texpine "Balancing F2P Economies" (2017, older)](https://texpine.com/media/files/Balancing%20Free-to-Play%20Economies.pdf)
- **Auction-house and trade fees** are among the largest sinks in MMO-style economies (attribution approximate). — [Machinations](https://machinations.io/articles/game-economy-design-free-to-play-games). In EVE Online's Oct 2024 report, **broker fees** entered the Top-8 faucets and sinks chart after the "Deathless Custodians" faucet dropped out. The Ship Index was down ~4.5% over 12 months and the Mineral Price Index hit a record 287. — [EVE Online MER, Oct 2024](https://www.eveonline.com/fr/news/view/monthly-economic-report-october-2024). EVE publishes faucet/sink totals monthly, with raw data downloads (the Nov 2024 sink/faucet total had a calculation error, corrected Dec 12). — [EVE Online MER, Nov 2024](https://www.eveonline.com/de/news/view/monthly-economic-report-november-2024)
- **Gold sinks** exist to remove currency, because excess currency inflates player-driven prices. — [Wikipedia: Gold sink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_sink)
- **Late game:** veterans accumulate more than they spend, so designers add new sinks (premium content, exclusive items) to absorb the surplus. Lifecycle: pre-launch spreadsheet simulation of faucet/sink ratios, soft-launch exposure of imbalances, live-ops tuning, then long-tail sinks. — [MWM glossary](https://mwm.ai/glossary/game-economy)
- **Segment monitoring:** "a whale's economy and a new player's economy inflate at completely different rates". Model flows pre-launch (e.g., Machinations, with Monte Carlo runs per a third-party claim), then monitor balances per segment. — [Game Growth Advisor, 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-07-14-game-economy-design-virtual-currency-balancing/)
- **Symptom of inflation:** an upgrade attainable last month now takes double the time or a different grind loop. — [PulseGeek](https://pulsegeek.com/articles/inflation-in-video-game-economies-causes-and-fixes/)
- **Axie Infinity (cautionary, one analyst's opinion):** its faucets had no adequate sinks, and new players' deposits paid old players' withdrawals. — [Yu-kai Chou](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-study/economy-design-framework-axie-infinity-collapse/)
- **Photo Finish $DERBY:** the developer's older economy deep dive assumed **1 DERBY ≈ $1** (a quasi-stablecoin). — [Third Time Games: Economy Deep Dive](https://thirdtimegames.com/?p=341). A later Rookie Rally description equates **$150 with 12,000 DERBY** (attribution approximate). — [Third Time Games](https://thirdtimegames.com/?p=4587)
- **"Monster horse" concentration** is named as the key problem (Feb 2025 community proposal). — [Bukidnon Farms proposal](https://bukidnon-farms-pfl.notion.site/Crown-and-Purse-Distribution-Proposal-Economic-Growth-now-18ff0c93488a806385adf433925e68d6). Zed Champions responds with **auto skill-matched racing**. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/316789/ethereum-nft-game-zed-run-reborn-base)
- **ZED RUN shutdown:** the original game was sunset on 28 February (year not shown in the snippet; context indicates 2025) in favour of Zed Champions, and **legacy horses did not transfer**. — [BlockchainGamer.biz](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/amp/news/37092/zed-run-discontinued-for-zed-champions/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/316789/ethereum-nft-game-zed-run-reborn-base)
- **Umamusume power creep** (newcomers have "a week of shelf life") is an inflation-like dynamic in unit power rather than currency. — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/i-spent-200-on-gold-city-in-umamusume-pretty-derby-so-you-dont-have-to/)

### Inferences
- **DERBY devaluation:** if both figures are accurate, 12,000 DERBY = $150 implies **~$0.0125 per DERBY**, roughly a **98–99% fall** from the $1 design assumption. This would be the clearest case in the genre of a currency losing value when emission (purses, stimulus, staking) outpaces sinks. It could also reflect a redenomination; I could not verify which.
- **Horse supply is the genre-specific inflation vector.** Breeding creates new assets every season. Sinks that retire horses (age, retirement, claiming, stud limits, breeding fees that partly burn) keep horse prices meaningful. ZED RUN's breeding-fee split (owner / prize pool / platform) is a model where part of the fee is removed from circulation.
- **For a chips/gems game:** (a) publish or monitor an internal monthly "faucet vs sink" ledger per segment (EVE-style), which a double-entry ledger makes cheap; (b) prefer **percentage** fees on player-to-player market and auction sales over flat fees; (c) keep **aspirational chip sinks** (expensive breeds, facilities, prestige items) for late game; (d) limit dominance with **brackets/classes** and skill matching so purses are not swept by a few horses.
- Because gems never convert to chips (and real money never directly creates chips), the chip economy stays closed. The inflation risk then comes only from **game faucets** (quests, achievements, season rewards, friend gifts, house-funded purses), so each new faucet should launch with a matching sink and a daily cap.
